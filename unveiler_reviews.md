Comments to the authorComments to author (Associate Editor)
## =====================================
The paper presents a framework for robotic manipulation in
cluttered environments that separates high-level spatial
reasoning from low-level action execution.
Even if the reviewers found a well-written and
well-structured paper, the main concerns regard the paper's
contribution, which looks incremental.
Moreover, some claims are not well supported. The authors
should better support the claimed ability to account for
grasp accessibility and scene stability. The zero-shot
transfer claim should be better supported, as the planar
grasping strategy relies on the robot's actual reachability
and kinematic constraints.
Please also refer to all the reviewers’ comments.

3 25797 The paper presents a well-structured and complete piece of
work with a clear motivation and comprehensive experiments.
The proposed Unveiler framework is conceptually sound, and
the overall idea of decoupling high-level spatial reasoning
from low-level manipulation is promising, but the
connection between several high-level claims and their
concrete technical realization and evaluation could be
clarified and better supported.
The Spatial Relationship Encoder (SRE) is described as
explicitly decomposing spatial reasoning; however, it is
supervised using the expert heuristic described in
Algorithm 1, which appears to combine only
distance-to-target and distance-to-boundary terms. Given
this heuristic, it would be helpful to further explain how
the model can exhibit the claimed ability to account for
grasp accessibility and scene stability. While the paper
mentions these spatial factors in real-world deployment,
the corresponding descriptions are too general to convey
how they are concretely implemented or evaluated.
While the Action Decoder fuses grasp heatmaps across
several discrete orientations to achieve rotation
invariance, this planar grasping strategy relies heavily on
the robot's actual reachability and kinematic constraints.
It would be helpful to further clarify how the real-world
system achieves zero-shot transfer, given the potential
differences between simulated and physical reachable
spaces. In particular, in the real-world setting,
determining the best object to remove should inherently
depend on the fixed arm's reachability constraints, which
are not modeled in the SRE's inputs.

- Limited Insight into Why the Approach Works:
- The paper reports strong results but provides limited

- There is little theoretical justification or formal

- Heavy Reliance on Heuristic Supervision:
- The training data is generated from a handcrafted
heuristic (Algorithms 1 and 2). Thus, the model essentially
learns to imitate a deterministic expert designed by the
authors.
- The “learning” component is therefore limited to
regression to known heuristics, not the discovery of new
strategies or emergent reasoning.
- Evaluation Scope and Depth:
- The real-world validation is minimal (a single robot,
small number of trials).
- No Clear Contribution to Broader Theory or Methodology:
- The claimed “object-centric spatial reasoning” is not
explicitly formalized.
- The paper does not propose a general algorithmic
principle that could extend beyond the specific
manipulation setting.
